How Live Houses Work in Japan: A First-Timer's Guide

A live house (ライブハウス) is a small or mid-sized music venue, usually standing-room and often in a basement. They're at the centre of Japan's music scene, and most bands play them for years. Going to one is simple, but a few customs can surprise first-time visitors.

Shows start early

Listings show two times: 開場 (kaijō) is when doors open and 開演 (kaien) is when the show starts, usually 30 minutes to an hour later. Evening shows commonly start between 18:30 and 19:30 and finish by about 21:30. Arriving at 21:00, as you might elsewhere, could mean missing most of it. Weekend shows may start earlier still.

The drink charge

Nearly every live house adds a compulsory drink charge on top of the ticket, typically around ¥500 to ¥800, paid in cash at the door. You'll usually get a token or ticket to exchange at the bar for one drink. Listings may show this as 1ドリンク別 (one drink extra). It isn't a mistake or a scam, and it applies even if you don't want a drink, though soft drinks are normally available too.

Entry order and ticket numbers

Many tickets carry an entry number called 整理番号 (seiri bangō). Staff call numbers in order at the door, often in groups of ten. A low number gets you closer to the stage, while a high number just means going in later. Arrive before doors open, listen for your number, and ask a nearby fan or a staff member if you're unsure. Some shows also have priority groups marked A or B, with all of A going in first.

What to bring

  • Cash: door tickets and drink charges are often cash only, even where tickets were sold online.
  • Your ticket and possibly ID: some larger shows check names against tickets.
  • Coins for a locker: many venues have small coin lockers, and there often isn't anywhere else for bags. For large bags, use a station locker before you arrive.

Etiquette inside

Photo and video rules vary. Many Japanese shows ban both, so look for signs or watch what others are doing. Audiences can seem quiet between songs, then respond in unison during the set. That's normal and doesn't mean people aren't enjoying it. Keep your bag small and hold it in front of you in a crowded room.

After the show

Bands often sell merchandise near the entrance, and members may come out to meet fans at smaller shows. Merch tables usually prefer cash. Check your last train before the show starts, especially if your route has a transfer.
